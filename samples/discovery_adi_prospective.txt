GoFresh shut down in 2022 — long story, mostly that the cold-chain wasn't ready and we were absorbing the losses. That's actually how I got obsessed with the cold-chain problem.

Karan: Why did GoFresh shut down specifically? Was it demand or supply side?

Adi: Honestly, supply. Restaurants wanted it. We could not deliver consistent product. We lost 38% of our inventory in the warehouse-to-restaurant leg, which is roughly twice what the operating model assumed. The cold-chain partners we worked with — three of the largest in Java — were operating sensors that were sometimes ten years old, with no predictive layer. By the time temperature alerts came in, the product was already spoiled.

Alina: So you saw the problem from the demand side first.

Adi: Yes. After GoFresh wound down, I spent six months consulting for two of the cold-chain operators, trying to understand if it was a hardware problem or a software problem. I concluded it was a software problem. The hardware exists. The data exists. Nobody has built the predictive layer because the major sensor vendors are Japanese and German, and their models don't account for Indonesian conditions — humidity spikes during monsoon, hours-long power cuts, port congestion that adds 18 hours to transit. Their models predict failures that don't happen and miss failures that do.

Karan: So you built the predictive layer.

Adi: Yes. We started in January 2024 — myself, two engineers from Gojek, and a data scientist from Tokopedia. We took a year to build the first model on a single agribusiness customer's data, with their permission. By December 2024 we had something that beat the incumbent prediction accuracy by 31 percentage points on the same data set. That's when we started commercial pilots.

Alina: What's the company called?
