Karan: The third one is the one that should sting. The first two are circumstantial. The third is structural.

Layla: To be fair to Tariq, he is not wrong. We tell every fellow the M1 in-person is non-negotiable, but the value of it is only obvious in retrospect. He's at week 4 of M2 and hasn't felt the compounding yet. Most fellows don't feel it until M3 or M4. By then he'd have been locked in.

Karan: That's the honest read. We have now lost two fellows on the relocation gate in twelve months. One in November, one this week. Both senior operators, both with families, both choosing capital over operating support.

Yousef: And it's worth noting — neither of them went to a worse outcome. The November fellow, Amira, went to Sequoia Surge. Tariq is going to Antler. They're not picking inferior alternatives. They're picking faster, lighter alternatives.

Layla: That's the real question. We should consider a hybrid M1 for the right profile. Two weeks in Doha — one for the foundational onboarding — and then two weeks remote with structured daily check-ins. Monthly in-person check-ins after. The current rigid model is filtering out exactly the senior operator profile we want.

Karan: I hear it. I'm not ready to commit to a policy change in this meeting. Let me think about it with Alina and bring a proposal to the next QDB review. The risk is — if we go hybrid, the on-site fellows feel like the standard is moving around them mid-cohort. Faisal, Naufal, the Egyptian fellow — they all relocated. We told them it was non-negotiable. If we suddenly say it's negotiable for the next cohort, what does that signal to them?

Yousef: I think you can frame it differently. The rigid M1 is for fellows who need the operating immersion. The hybrid M1 is for senior operators who have already built companies and need different support — board readiness, fundraising mechanics, hiring at scale. Two products for two profiles.

Karan: That's a more defensible frame. But it requires us to be willing to admit publicly that some fellows need different things than others. The current marketing line is "every fellow goes through the same Co-Build." That's been a strength.

Layla: It's a strength when you're recruiting first-time founders. It's a weakness when you're recruiting senior operators. We need to decide which audience we're optimizing for.

Karan: Or both. That's what Yousef is suggesting.

Yousef: Yes. Both, with explicit tracks.
